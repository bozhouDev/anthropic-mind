## 2025-10-12 20:15:15 · jackclarkSF · 1977468126968287459

Both may end up being true: there will be a small number of "low friction" companies which can deploy AI at maximal scale and speed (these will be the frontier AI companies themselves, as well as some tech startups, and perhaps a few major non-tech enterprises) and I think these companies will see massive ramps in success on pretty much ~every dimension, and then there will be a much larger blob of "high friction" companies and organizations where diffusion is grindingly slow due to a mixture of organizational culture, as well as many, many, many papercuts accrued from things like internal data handling policies / inability to let AI systems 'see' across the entire organization, etc.

## 2025-10-12 20:16:52 · jackclarkSF · 1977468533299949585

My mindset is we're going to see a takeoff in some number of actors in the economy and it'll seem almost like they're time traveling relative to other businesses. Then there will eventually be a takeoff in the rest of the economy after it becomes clear that you need to adopt this stuff or you become out-competed. My general recommendation to policymakers is going through the exercise of asking 'what would it take to deploy AI to every single person in the organization?' is a helpful question to ask, because this usually highlights all the impediments to technology diffusion broadly in a given organization/company
