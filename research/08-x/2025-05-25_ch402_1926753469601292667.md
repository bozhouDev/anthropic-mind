## 2025-05-25 21:34:45 · ch402 · 1926753831884296505

One thing which I think is a very deep consequence of the transformer is the ability of attention heads to compose and form mechanisms like induction heads.

We know that transformers outperform LSTMs because they perform better as the context gets longer (see Kaplan et al Fig 7).

At least in small models, induction heads drive this difference (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html ). (They also explain the scaling law bump in Kaplan et al Fig 1a.)

## 2025-05-25 21:35:52 · ch402 · 1926754112273596912

Zooming out to your question more broadly, one thing I'd note is that many operations in the transformer have different, equivalent mathematical formulations which are easier to reason about. In particular:

- Attention is a lot easier to reason about if you split up the attention equations into a sum over heads.
- Attention is cleaner if you think of W_O and W_V as being a single low rank W_OV, and similarly for W_Q and W_K as a single W_QK
- I personally like a tensor formulation of all of this.

This is all discussed in https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html
