## 2025-04-10 18:13:17 · AmandaAskell · 1910395678649684346

This positive spiral of doom is easy to fall into because success *should* increase your confidence in your own views and *should* decrease your confidence in others, but a slight lack of perspective on your part will cause your evidence to seem stronger and stronger over time.

## 2025-04-10 18:13:17 · AmandaAskell · 1910395677345214641

This is compounded by the fact that success causes others to trust you more, to second guess their own instinct to question you, and to believe that failures are more likely to be caused by factors other than you. So your evidence in your own abilities only seems to increase.

## 2025-04-10 18:13:17 · AmandaAskell · 1910395676254756967

The problem is that if you become miscalibrated about how reliable and general your abilities are, or how representative your prior detractors have been, you make it harder for new detractors to get through to you at all. You increasingly can't be recalibrated by good detractors.

## 2025-04-10 18:13:17 · AmandaAskell · 1910395675256483940

It can be very hard to get through to people with success-induced blindness. If you gamble on your own views despite others saying you're wrong and you're repeatedly proven right, you start to give less and less weight to your detractors over time. This isn't wholly irrational.
