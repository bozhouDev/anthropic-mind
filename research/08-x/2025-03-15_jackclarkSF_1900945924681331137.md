## 2025-03-15 16:23:20 · jackclarkSF · 1900945924681331137

Important result - distributed training of Chinchilla-style LLMs out to 10B parameters gets you a LOWER loss than standard data parallel training if training across two replicas (M=2). For AI policy, the key question is if M can go above something like ~10. https://x.com/jackclarkSF/status/1900945924681331137

## 2025-03-15 16:23:52 · jackclarkSF · 1900946058769035391

M=2 basically means 'two datacenters is better than one' for training sizeable models. M=10 would mean 'ten very small data centers is better than one big one' - which would have significant AI policy implications. We aren't seeing that yet.

## 2025-03-15 16:24:43 · jackclarkSF · 1900946271449629148

The other key thing is model sizes - the true test of distributed training is if someone can make a ~80b parameter model trained across more than 10 sites. If that happens I think a lot of the assumptions in parts of AI policy start breaking.
